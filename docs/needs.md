# Who it is for, and what they need

## The person

A friend of iiSurf's who grew up speaking Ukrainian. They speak English very well but have a strong Ukrainian accent, and they want to pronounce English more like an American speaker. Their name, and anything else that could identify them, stays out of this repository.

**How we found out.** Articulate is a surprise, so iiSurf answered these questions for them on October 30, 2026, from knowing them well. Three questions had no answer. At the handover we will ask them directly, and log what changed.

## Sounds that are often hard for Ukrainian speakers of English

These are common patterns, not a diagnosis of our friend. Sources: [Halahan, Bezuhla, and Leshchinska (2023)](https://philol.vernadskyjournals.in.ua/journals/2023/1_2023/part_1/23.pdf) and [TEFL teaching notes](https://teflcourse.net/blog/pronunciation-problems-in-ukraine).

- "th" said as t, d, s, z, or f: think, this, three.
- "w" said as "v": west and vest, wine and vine. The Ukrainian в sits between the two.
- Unstressed syllables said in full, without the small "uh": about, banana, today. Ukrainian keeps every vowel clear, so this is one of the biggest giveaways.
- Short and long vowels: ship and sheep, full and fool. Also bed and bad.
- Gliding vowels said flat: "go" and "coat" with a plain о, so coat sounds like caught.
- p, t, and k without the small puff of air, so pin can sound like bin.
- The English r, and the "ng" at the end of sing.
- Unlike many Russian speakers, Ukrainian speakers usually keep b, d, and g voiced at the end of a word, so bad and bat are less often a problem.
